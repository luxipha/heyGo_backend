# Driver Home and matching backend contract

## Connection and availability

`GET /driver/dashboard` returns `onboarding`, `eligibleToDrive`, `availability` (`status`, `available`, `onlineRequested`), `map.driverLocation`, `activeOffer`, `activeTrip`, `todayEarnings`, and `operatingBalance`. The map location is the last accepted driver position or `null`. `activeOffer` contains `tripId`, `attempt`, and `expiresAt` while an unexpired offer is pending. The Home screen has two distinct money values: accrued **Today's Earnings** and **Operating Balance**.

Driver Earnings are accrued at trip completion, even when the rider pays the driver directly. Each completed trip records the driver-attributable fare minus the HeyGo commission in `driver_trip_earnings`, independently of payment settlement or payout. HeyGo commission is currently 0%, so a ₦5,000 trip records ₦5,000 of Driver Earnings. The transaction records the fare, commission rate and amount, earnings amount, and completion time once per trip. `todayEarnings` aggregates `fareKobo`, `commissionKobo`, `amountKobo`, and `completedTrips` for the Africa/Lagos calendar date; it also reports `date`, `timeZone`, and `currency: "NGN"`. An empty day returns zero amounts and zero trips.

A driver may open `/ws/drivers` while offline. The socket sends `driver.cmd.register` with the saved HeyGo profile and vehicle identity; opening it does not go online. The app sends `driver.cmd.location` through this socket before calling `POST /driver/availability` with `{ "available": true }`. The server verifies seven completed requirements, staff approval, the selected vehicle package, a GPS reading from the last 30 minutes, the GPS-derived approved market, and its approved minimum-balance policy. A missing policy blocks Go Online even when the balance is zero; an approved zero minimum permits it. Matching also rejects a GPS reading older than 30 minutes. Calling the endpoint with `false`, losing the socket, or losing approval clears online intent. The response reflects persisted `status`, `available`, and `onlineRequested`; `available` is temporarily false during an offer or trip even if online was requested.

The current Driver Flutter controller still treats socket connection as online and has local inspection and package state. It needs to call the availability endpoint and use the dashboard and onboarding responses before the backend flow is usable through the app.

## Operating Balance

Operating Balance is a dedicated balance used to pay applicable charges after a completed trip. It is separate from the driver's trip earnings and payout balance. Before going online and before sending a new offer, the server must require both the existing seven onboarding requirements with HeyGo staff approval and an Operating Balance at or above the configured minimum. A low balance must never interrupt an active trip. Once the trip finishes and its charge is applied, a balance below the minimum blocks the next offer until a confirmed top-up restores eligibility.

The minimum balance is set in Admin, along with the low balance warning, whether negative balances are allowed, the maximum negative balance, top-up minimum and maximum, and the applicable market. The displayed ₦2,000 minimum, ₦3,000 warning, and ₦500 negative limit are examples only; none is a backend default. Statutory charge rates and formulas are also versioned Admin configuration, created by one HeyGo staff account and approved by another, with charge bearer and funding source recorded per rule. Admin can create, review, approve, and retire versioned minimum-balance policies using `/admin/operating-balance/policies`; a different staff account must approve each change. New drafts require `topupMinimumKobo` and `topupMaximumKobo`. Previously approved policies without these values remain usable for Go Online, but cannot initiate top-up checkout until replaced. Admin can query `GET /admin/operating-balance/diagnostics?marketCode=LAGOS` for `MINIMUM_BALANCE_POLICY_MISSING`. Drivers receive a generic area message instead.

The socket stores GPS while the driver is offline. Each new position resolves an operating market and refreshes dispatch eligibility immediately. Matching requires the driver's current market to equal the trip's frozen pickup market, along with the balance policy check. `GET /driver/operating-balance` and the dashboard return `balanceKobo`, `marketCode`, `locationFresh`, `minimumKobo`, `warningKobo`, `topupMinimumKobo`, `topupMaximumKobo`, `canGoOnline`, and `lowBalance`. A missing policy gives null policy values; zero balance represents an account with no posted entries.

`POST /driver/operating-balance/topups` requires a signed CasperID business token with an `email` claim, an `Idempotency-Key` header, and `{ "amountKobo": 500000 }`. The backend uses the latest GPS market only when its reading is less than 30 minutes old, then enforces the market's approved `topupMinimumKobo` and `topupMaximumKobo`. The payment service records a pending top-up before initiating Monnify checkout; the response contains `id`, `checkoutUrl`, `paymentReference`, `transactionReference`, `marketCode`, `amountKobo`, and `status: "pending"`. `GET /driver/operating-balance/topups/:id` reads a driver-owned top-up's current status. Monnify checkout redirects use the configured `MONNIFY_REDIRECT_URL`; the app must read the backend status rather than treating that redirect as confirmation. The internal gateway-to-payment route uses `INTERNAL_SERVICE_TOKEN`, and local development sets `PAYMENT_SERVICE_URL=http://localhost:9200`.

The Monnify webhook path authenticates the notification, verifies the transaction server-side, checks reference, `PAID` status, NGN currency and exact amount, and posts a single append-only credit and account update under database locks. A repeated transaction reference returns the already-posted result. Confirmed top-ups repay the oldest unpaid driver-funded statutory charges before leaving any remainder in Operating Balance. Versioned statutory-rule Admin routes, calculation, post-trip snapshots/debits, and eligibility checks are implemented and covered by local PostGIS tests. No legal charge rules are seeded. The `email` claim must be supplied by CasperID in the signed business token; the current live CasperID token and payment sandbox have not been exercised. Production-provider and deployed multi-service verification remain outstanding. Rider/platform-funded rules may be drafted but cannot be approved until their settlement collection flow is implemented.

## Driver route

`POST /routes/driver` requires a driver JWT and a currently assigned, accepted, or started trip:

```json
{
  "tripId": "<trip UUID>",
  "origin": { "latitude": 6.45, "longitude": 3.39 },
  "destination": { "latitude": 6.5, "longitude": 3.4 }
}
```

The response `data` has `tripId`, `polyline` (precision 5), `distanceMeters`, `etaSeconds`, and `maneuvers` (`type`, `modifier`, `roadName`, `location`, `distanceMeters`, `durationSeconds`). It uses the configured `OSRM_API` base URL, shared with trip fare routing. Routing has not been exercised against a live OSRM service in this workspace.

## Offers and decisions

`driver.cmd.trip_request` contains the existing `trip` object (route and selected fare included) plus `offer: { attempt, expiresAt }`. The rider name and photo remain absent until CasperID supplies the exact profile contract; no display values are fabricated.

Driver socket commands `driver.cmd.trip_accept` and `driver.cmd.trip_decline` receive `driver.event.command_acknowledged` only after the owning service processes the action. The event includes `command`, `tripId`, and `status` (`accepted`, `declined`, or `rejected` for an unavailable acceptance). The gateway no longer reports Kafka delivery as successful trip acceptance. The offer-expiry worker emits `trip.event.expired` to the previous driver; a successful subsequent match emits `trip.event.reassigned` to that driver and the rider. Each of those events includes `tripId` and `status`. Driver events use the existing event ID and replay contracts.

The outbox, database ordering, and WebSocket takeover/replay integration tests pass locally against disposable PostGIS and Kafka services. The takeover test runs two independent gateway handlers sharing Postgres; a 500-socket local run measured the 250 ms lease-aware polling query. Separate-process staging verification, OSRM, and mobile flows still need a configured environment. Payment and location notifications remain outside the trip outbox and need a direct-publication audit.

## Trip reads and route paths

`GET /driver/trips/:tripID` returns a trip assigned to the authenticated driver, including persisted status, route, fare in kobo, arrival/start/completion timestamps, cancellation reason, and accrued earnings when completed. `GET /driver/trips?from=&to=&status=&cursor=&limit=` provides cursor-paginated assigned-driver history, including cancellation actor, no-show claim/debt state, settlement status, rating and feedback tags. `GET /driver/trips/:tripID/receipt` returns a read-only receipt for completed trips with fare/debt breakdown and the driver's manually-entered settlement state. Driver-authored rating uses `POST /trips/:tripID/rating` with a 1–5 score and up to three unique tags: `clean_and_tidy`, `easy_pickup`, and `great_chat`; written comments are rejected on that path.

Rider-authored rating uses `POST /rider/trips/:tripID/rating` with a 1–5 score and optional `comment` (trimmed, maximum 250 Unicode characters). Rider comments cannot include Driver feedback tags. A one-star Rider rating of the assigned Driver is flagged as `pending` for HeyGo staff review. The rated Driver reads their Rider-submitted ratings/comments from cursor-paginated `GET /driver/reviews?cursor=&limit=`. HeyGo staff reads the queue from `GET /admin/driver-ratings?status=pending|reviewed|all&cursor=&limit=` and marks a pending one-star review reviewed with `POST /admin/driver-ratings/:tripID/review`; review actions are recorded in the Admin audit log. Admin routes require an authenticated HeyGo staff session, and the review mutation requires CSRF protection. Both the Driver and authorized staff can read Rider comments; Rider-authored comments are not included in the Driver's rating submission flow.

The authenticated Driver can read an optional next-of-kin contact from `GET /driver/safety-contacts` and create or replace it with `PUT /driver/safety-contacts` (`fullName`, `relationship`, `phoneNumber`). Phone values are normalized to E.164; Nigerian local 11-digit mobile numbers are accepted and normalized with the `+234` country code. The contact is private to its owning Driver account.

`GET /driver/performance` returns all-time completed-trip count, Rider-submitted rating average/count, and the Driver's all-time rating percentile. Percentile compares Drivers with at least 25 Rider ratings; the response reports the eligible cohort size and withholds the percentile below that threshold. `GET /driver/reviews?cursor=&limit=` returns the Driver's Rider-submitted ratings and optional comments. Performance, reviews, and next-of-kin APIs are backend implemented; Driver app integration remains separate work.

`POST /trips/:tripID/arrival` queues the arrival command and `trip.event.arrived` is committed through the trip outbox. Start Trip remains an explicit driver action. Completion remains asynchronous and returns `202 accepted`; it atomically creates a pending direct-payment settlement for the rounded fare and emits completed and settlement-updated events. The authenticated driver can read `GET /driver/trips/:tripID/settlement`, manually confirm with `POST /driver/trips/:tripID/settlement/confirm`, or dispute with `POST /driver/trips/:tripID/settlement/disputes`. No bank/provider verification is claimed; the first terminal action wins and repeated identical actions are idempotent. The existing `POST /routes/driver` is used on demand; no route-update event is persisted. Driver app wiring for rating tags, history, receipts, arrival/settlement, and live multi-service verification remain outstanding. Unused singular lifecycle aliases have been removed; `/trip/preview` and `/trip/start` remain because `apps/web` calls them.

Fare previews persist the rider-requested pickup and destination coordinates separately from OSRM's snapped route geometry. At trip creation, the backend requires exactly one approved market geofence for pickup and exactly one approved jurisdiction geofence for each endpoint. It also checks airport geofences at both endpoints. A missing required match or any overlapping match rejects creation with `trip_market_unavailable`; no driver or rider can choose the classification. The trip stores market and origin/destination region codes, `LOCAL` or `INTERSTATE`, optional `AIRPORT_PICKUP` and `AIRPORT_DROPOFF` tags, and the IDs and versions of matching geofences. Admin geofence create/list/detail/approval/retirement routes are present and require two distinct staff accounts for publication or retirement. Statutory rule Admin routes and evaluation remain pending. Until approved geofences are configured, trip creation fails closed.
